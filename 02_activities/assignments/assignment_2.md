Bad Visualization:

    - Explain (with reference to material covered up to date, along with readings and other scholarly sources, as needed) why you classified that visualization the way you did.
    
    1- The visualization relies heavily on hue to distinguish categories, but once you introduce 10+ overlapping colors, the result becomes cognitively overwhelming.
    2- The color palette likely fails common color-blindness standards. There is also no secondary encoding to reinforce meaning beyond color and on top of that, small fonts and labels placed inside dense visual regions reduce readibility.
    3- There are too many flows overlap at the same time, with excessive crossings that make it nearly impossible to follow a single path from beginning to end and there's no clear focal point.
    4- It's not clear if the width of each flow represents actual quantitative values or is simply decorative.
    
    - How could this data visualization have been improved?  
    
    1- Split the content into multiple diagrams, each focused on a primary pathway and if possible add interactive filtering so flows can be isolated.
    2- Use color-blind-safe palette, reinforce color with patters or line styles, increase label size, and maintain sufficient contrast.
    3- Redesign the layout to reduce line crossings and overlapsm provide a clear legend explaining what each visual encoding means, and establish a hierarchy by making the most important flows visually dominant.
    4- Rethink the chart and question whether a Sankey diagram is the right format at all.
